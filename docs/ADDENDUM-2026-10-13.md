## A1. Switch date of the timestamp convention (task 2a) - script `followup/code/switch_date.py`

Window: weeks from Monday 2018-10-15 to Friday 2019-03-29 (covers the last New-York-style week the pack found, 2 Nov 2018, and the first European-style week, 15 Mar 2019, with margin).

For each pair (ten FX pairs; XAUUSD and XAGUSD) and each trading day in the window:
1. Last bar label before each weekend (Friday) and first bar label after it (Sunday), and for metals the last label before and first label after the daily break (largest gap of 30 minutes or more starting between 15:00 and 19:00 label time).
2. Prediction of each convention for those labels: New York wall clock gives close 16:59 / reopen 18:00 (metals) in every week; EU-calendar clock gives 15:59 / 17:00 in weeks when the US is on daylight time and the EU is not, and 16:59 / 18:00 otherwise.
3. A day is "informative" only if the two conventions predict different labels (US and EU on different time). Days when both predict the same label are "uninformative" and carry no information about which convention applies.
4. For the three pairs with FXCM (UTC-stamped) candles, weekly correlation of one-minute bid returns at label +4 h, +5 h, +6 h; the weekly best offset is compared with each convention's predicted offset. Same informative / uninformative split.
5. Result reported: the last informative day consistent only with New York style, the first informative day consistent only with EU style, per instrument; the narrowest interval supported by ALL instruments; and a statement of how many calendar days inside that interval are uninformative by construction.
6. Provider-change probe (exploratory, secondary): for EURUSD, USDJPY, AUDUSD, weekly mean and standard deviation of the log difference between histdata close and FXCM bid close (bp) at the correct offset, weekly correlation, weekly share of zero-return histdata bars, weekly mean bar range, from 2018-09-01 to 2019-04-30. Break search: for each candidate week, absolute Welch t between the 8 weeks before and the 8 weeks after; report the maximum and its week. No claim of a date unless the maximum is clearly isolated; a candidate of 16 Dec 2018 (stated by a GitHub write-up) is examined only as one of the candidate weeks, not as a target.

## A2. Why the artefact begins in 2015 (task 2b) - script `followup/code/break_2015.py`

Raw histdata files 2013-2016, ten FX pairs, true clock (two-era; the era question does not arise before 2019), one pair-year at a time. Monthly series per pair:
- mean and t of the 16:59 New York bar return (one observation per trading day);
- share of trading days on which the 16:59 bar return is exactly zero;
- number of bars per month (quote-count proxy) and number of bars in 16:55-17:04 New York;
- mean bar range (bp) of bars 16:30-16:54 and of the 16:59 bar;
- share of all bars with high = low (very few ticks in the minute);
- number of decimal places used (max digits after the point in the open price, sample of 20,000 bars per month);
- fraction of days with no bar between 17:00 and 17:04 New York (daily gap position proxy).
Break test per pair: for each candidate month b from 2014-07 to 2015-12, absolute Welch t between the daily 16:59 returns of the 12 months before b and the 6 months from b; the estimated break month is the one maximising it. The same break statistic is computed for each other series above (monthly means), to see whether any coincides. Coincidence criterion fixed in advance: within one month of the 16:59 break month for at least 8 of 10 pairs.
Public record: fetch the vendor's public pages (home, FAQ, about, news/forum if any) and the Wayback Machine CDX index for those pages if reachable, look for wording about data source, feed or provider changes in 2014-2016. Report what is there verbatim, or that nothing is.

## A3. Robustness (task 3) - script `followup/code/robustness.py`

1. Minute-of-day profile (true clock, 2015-2026 and 2009-2014) for every FX pair, gold, silver on disk (step 1 and 2 outputs), and for the seven further series on disk (GRXEUR, JPXJPY, NSXUSD, SPXUSD, UDXUSD, UKXGBP, WTIUSD) after running steps through a New-York profile built the same way (two-era clock assumed, labelled as an assumption; the clock diagnosis is run on each file first and reported).
2. Size by year: for each instrument and year, mean, t and share-negative of the 16:59 New York bar, and the sum 16:55-17:04.
3. Masks on the rule "short 16:00-17:00" (true clock), grid fixed now: mask start b minutes before 17:00 with b in {0 (none), 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30}; reported for 2015-2026, ten FX pairs. Because the rule ends at 17:00, minutes after 17:00 do not enter it. The 16:00-16:29 part (residual of a 30-minute mask) is reported separately as the "early-hour residual".
4. Asymmetry: the companion rule "long NY hour 18 (18:00-19:00)" is NOT computable beyond 18:29 from the stored window, so instead the after-side of the mask is tested on the stored window 16:30-18:29: the sum of returns from 17:05 to 18:29 and from 18:00 to 18:29, with masks [17:00-5, 17:00+a) for a in {5, 35, 65, 89}. Residual = mean, t.
5. Criterion fixed in advance: a mask "removes the residual" for a pair if |t| < 2 for the rule in 2015-2026. All pairs and all grid points are reported; no selection of the best mask. With 10 pairs x 7 masks, a few |t| < 2 by chance are expected under zero residual; a mask is called "effective" only if at least 8 of 10 pairs satisfy |t| < 2 at that and every wider b.
6. Whether any remaining residual is genuine seasonality or more spread drift cannot be settled from bid-only data; the FXCM mid for three pairs is used only to the extent already in the pack (F3). No further claim.

## A1b. Added later the same day, still before any switch-date result was computed: provider-change probes for the era boundary

Reason: the clock labels cannot date the switch inside the 18 weeks when New York and Europe are both on winter time (the two conventions give identical output there). A public write-up (radiusred/tradedesk-marketdata issue #93, comment of 9 Oct 2026, archived in `followup\archive\`) says the provider changed on the weekend of 14-16 Dec 2018 and cites two data properties of the SPXUSD tick file: the share of quotes on a 0.25-point grid (82-93% before, 0% after) and the time of Friday's last tick (16:59 before, 16:14 after). These are that write-up's claims; the following tests check them independently on the M1 files on disk and do not assume them.
Script `followup/code/switch_date.py`, extra outputs:
1. For SPXUSD, NSXUSD, UKXGBP, GRXEUR, JPXJPY, UDXUSD, WTIUSD and the ten FX pairs and two metals, per calendar day from 2018-10-01 to 2019-03-31: share of bars whose close lies on the 0.25 grid (indices) or on the 0.00005 / 0.0001 grid (FX, by last-digit pattern), number of bars, label of the first and last bar of the day. Per instrument, change-point = the single day maximising the absolute difference in the daily grid share between the 10 days before and 10 days after.
2. Report each instrument's change-point and whether the instruments agree. If they agree within a few days, that date is reported as the date of a feed change, and the clock switch is described as "at or before the first week with the new feed's properties and after 2 Nov 2018", with the inference stated as an inference (the clock rule follows the feed in the write-up's account; this analysis cannot test that link inside winter).
